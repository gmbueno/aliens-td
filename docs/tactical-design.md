# Tactical systems: choices with visible consequences

Research checked on November 7, 2026. Bloons TD6 uses three upgrade paths, with a principal high-tier path and restricted secondary upgrades rather than allowing every upgrade together. Community reference: [Crosspathing](https://bloons.fandom.com/wiki/Crosspathing). Ninja Kiwi's [official update notes](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?appgroupname=Bloons+TD+6&appids=960090&enddate=1760487838&feed=steam_community_announcements) describe how changing crosspath bonuses creates different roles, and how support and economy paths trade speed, output and interactions. These informed the design; this game does not copy their full upgrade system.

For our UAVs the rule is deliberately smaller: damage and firing-speed core improvements, then **one** specialization, Cryo or Incendiary, up to three ranks. Buying a rank immediately locks the opposite specialization on that UAV. Each UAV has its own independent branch, upgrades, target priority and landing tile. The slow path helps concentrated damage; the burn path helps sustained cleanup. Slow immunity remains meaningful. Damage is doubled; Motors affects rate, not reach.

Factories trade a weapon slot, placement tile and up-front funds for later combat income. Four base payouts per wave, eight seconds apart, $15 each; upgrades improve payout or production/cap. No idle, pause or intermission income, and no instant payout on placement. This prevents an indefinite last-alien stall from becoming an unlimited money generator.

Siege Missiles commit to a visible, movable impact point and resolve delayed explosions there, including aliens that cross later. Mine Foundries convert nearby path cells into persistent finite defenses. Gas covers every ground alien in its cell-based radius but does not stop flyers. Sky Wraiths take the direct entry-to-exit line; neither ground mines nor gas can damage them. Optional infested cargo frees land for no cash but creates three real enemies with the usual rewards and leak damage.

Tile range uses the same cell-center membership for preview and acquisition. A Priority Mark overrides automatic target ordering only for weapons that aim and only while the enemy remains eligible/in range; it cannot steer fixed-point missiles, gas, or mines. Fastest uses current speed, including rage, haste, ice, stun and slow.

## Distinct directions for later iterations

1. **Extraction objectives:** defend a rescue shuttle for three waves, then choose to leave safely or risk another wave for a better reward. A short goal inside endless play, without another currency.
2. **Breakable boss systems:** disable a shield generator or engine before damaging the main body. Enemy inspection and Priority Mark already support the interaction.
3. **Map switches:** activate a gate to redirect the next wave through a different lane. Visible consequence and an explicit one-wave countdown; no additional loadout complexity.
4. **Power-grid terrain:** a few connected powered tiles improve support towers, while enemies can temporarily disable a generator. This adds geography rather than more weapon variants.
5. **Optional combat contracts:** three clear per-run tasks such as “clear the cargo without a breach” or “survive with two factories”. Reward existing XP, not a new resource.
6. **Mutually exclusive mission rewards:** choose one temporary rule after a free-play milestone: stronger mines, higher factory yield, or longer power cooldown recovery. Three illustrated options, one choice.
7. **Boss telegraphs:** show the incoming attack zone before a boss disables a tower. A useful decision can be a short-duration protection power, rather than demanding constant repositioning.
8. **Replayable handcrafted challenges:** short scenarios with a fixed budget and roster, using the current economy, flying enemies and optional encounters. Clear win condition and no permanent progression penalty.

These are proposals, not features claimed to be implemented in this release. Prioritize extraction goals, boss systems and route switches; each changes what the player does rather than just adding another stat upgrade.
